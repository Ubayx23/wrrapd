## The knots (id="knots")
- Eyebrow: the five knots
- Heading: What's loud in your head right now?
- Intro: Pick the one that sounds most like you today. That's where you start.

### 1. Overthinking
- Sounds like: "Let me think about it one more time."
- What's happening: You're not solving the problem. You're replaying it. A thought that keeps repeating isn't giving you new information. It's asking for your attention, and you keep giving it.
- Ask yourself: If I already knew the answer, what would I do next?
- Do this now: Write the thought down in one sentence. Under it, write "I'll come back to this at 7pm." Close the notebook.

### 2. The future
- Sounds like: "What if it all goes wrong?"
- What's happening: Your mind is living in a day that doesn't exist yet. It rehearses the worst version so you feel prepared, but all it really does is make you live it twice.
- Ask yourself: What is the one step in front of me today?
- Do this now: Split a page in two. Left: what I can act on this week. Right: what I can't control. Do one thing from the left side. Leave the right side on the page.

### 3. Holding back
- Sounds like: "I'll start when I'm ready."
- What's happening: Not starting feels safe, because you can't fail at something you never tried. But waiting to feel ready is just fear with good manners.
- Ask yourself: What would I start this week if no one would ever see it fail?
- Do this now: Do the smallest version of it in the next ten minutes. One paragraph. One message. One rep.

### 4. Attachment
- Sounds like: "I'll be okay once I have it."
- What's happening: Wanting turned into needing. Now the thing you want is holding your peace hostage, and every delay feels like a threat.
- Ask yourself: If I never got this, who would I still be?
- Do this now: Think of what you want and clench your fist. Feel how tight that is. Now open your hand and keep thinking about it. You still want it. You just stopped strangling it. Now do one thing today that moves you toward it.

### 5. Self-sabotage
- Sounds like: "Why do I keep doing this?"
- What's happening: It isn't laziness. Some part of you is protecting you from something: being seen, being judged, or losing the version of you that you already know.
- Ask yourself: What am I afraid would happen if I actually followed through?
- Do this now: Finish this sentence on paper: "The part of me that stops me is trying to protect me from..."

## How it works (id="how")
- Eyebrow: how it works
- Heading: Learn it. Write it. Notice it.
1. Learn it: Each lesson takes one knot apart. What it is, why your mind does it, how it shows up in you, and what to do about it. Five minutes, no fluff.
2. Write it: Every lesson ends with one question for your notebook. Paper, not a screen. You remember what you write by hand, and nobody reads it but you.
3. Notice it: After a while, you catch the knot while it's forming. That's the whole skill. Seeing it from the outside is what loosens it.

## The practice
- Eyebrow: the practice
- Heading: Five lines, every night.
- The five lines: what happened / what I thought / what I felt / what I did / what the observer notices
- Body: When something hits you, don't analyze it. Write it in five lines. The first four are the story. The fifth is you stepping out of the story and looking at it. That step back is the practice.
- Body 2: Seneca did a version of this every night. Before sleep, he went back over his whole day, without excuses and without punishing himself. Just looking.

## FAQ (id="faq")
- Eyebrow: before you begin
- Heading: Fair questions.

Why is wrrapd free?
Because the ideas aren't mine to sell. Most of what's here comes from the Stoics, the Buddha and modern psychology, and some of it is over two thousand years old. I'm putting it in one place and making it easier to use. Something meant to make your mind lighter shouldn't come with a bill.

What's the catch?
There isn't one. No ads, no selling your data, no premium tier waiting at lesson six. If wrrapd ever needs money to keep running, there will be a donate button, and it will always be optional.

What can I expect?
Short lessons, each on one knot in your thinking. Some will feel obvious until you write about them. Expect a few uncomfortable questions. Don't expect a fix overnight. You're learning to see your own mind, and that takes reps.

Why paper and not an app?
Writing by hand slows you down enough to actually think. It's also private in a way nothing online can be. wrrapd teaches you. Your notebook is where the work happens.

Who is this for?
People who live in their heads. Who replay conversations, rehearse the future, and know exactly what they should do but don't do it. If that's you, start with the knot that's loudest today.

Is this therapy?
No. wrrapd teaches how the mind works, the way a good book would. If you're going through something heavy, please talk to a licensed professional. Learning about your mind and getting support aren't either-or.
